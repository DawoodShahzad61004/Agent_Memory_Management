# Omega-3 Fatty Acids and Inflammation

Long-chain omega-3 fatty acids, chiefly EPA and DHA, are incorporated into cell
membranes and act as precursors to specialised pro-resolving mediators, the
signalling molecules that actively shut inflammation down rather than merely
blocking its start.

In animal models of induced arthritis, omega-3 enriched diets reduce joint
swelling and lower circulating inflammatory markers. The effect sizes in these
rodent studies are large and consistent.

Human evidence is weaker. A meta-analysis of randomised trials in rheumatoid
arthritis found a small but reproducible reduction in patient-reported joint
pain and in use of anti-inflammatory drugs, with no detectable effect on
radiographic joint damage.

Large cardiovascular outcome trials of low-dose fish oil have been mostly
negative. The trials showing benefit used high-dose purified EPA rather than
mixed EPA/DHA preparations, which is one reason results across the literature
appear to conflict.
