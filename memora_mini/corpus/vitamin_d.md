# Vitamin D and Bone Density

Vitamin D governs intestinal calcium absorption. Without adequate vitamin D the
gut absorbs only a fraction of dietary calcium, and the body maintains serum
calcium by drawing on bone, which lowers bone mineral density over time.

A meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials found that vitamin D taken
alone had little effect on fracture rate in community-dwelling adults. The same
analysis found a modest reduction in hip fracture when vitamin D was combined
with calcium, particularly in institutionalised older adults with low baseline
intake.

Randomised trials of high intermittent dosing tell a different story: very large
annual doses were associated with an increase in falls and fractures rather than
a decrease. Dose schedule appears to matter as much as total dose.

Rodent studies of dietary vitamin D restriction show clear reductions in
trabecular bone volume within weeks. These animal-model results describe a
mechanism; they are not evidence of a fracture benefit in humans and should not
be quoted as such.
