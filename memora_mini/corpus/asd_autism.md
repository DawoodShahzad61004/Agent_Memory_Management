# ASD in Developmental Psychiatry

In developmental psychiatry, ASD stands for Autism Spectrum Disorder. It is a
neurodevelopmental condition characterised by persistent differences in social
communication and by restricted, repetitive patterns of behaviour or interests.
Diagnosis is behavioural: there is no blood test and no imaging marker.

Prevalence estimates from large population surveillance studies sit at roughly
1 in 100 children worldwide, though reported rates vary substantially by
country and by the case-definition used. These are observational figures drawn
from registry and screening data, not from randomised trials.

Onset is early. Most diagnostic criteria require that traits be present in the
early developmental period, even where they are only recognised later, once
social demands exceed the person's coping strategies. Diagnosis in adulthood is
common and does not imply late onset.

Support needs vary widely across the spectrum. Two people with the same
diagnostic label can have very different day-to-day support requirements, which
is why current criteria record a severity level for social communication and for
restricted or repetitive behaviour separately.
