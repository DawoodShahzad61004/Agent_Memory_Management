# Reading Evidence Grades

Not all study designs support the same claims, and the grade of evidence should
travel with any statement drawn from this corpus.

An animal model shows a mechanism in a non-human organism. It can explain how
something might work. It cannot establish that the effect occurs in people, or
at what dose.

An observational study reports what happened in a population that was not
randomised. It can establish association and is often the only ethical design
available, but confounding limits any causal reading.

A randomised controlled trial, or RCT, assigns the intervention by chance,
which removes confounding by design. A single RCT is still one result, in one
population, at one dose schedule.

A meta-analysis pools comparable trials. It gives the most stable estimate of
effect size available, but it inherits every bias present in the trials it
pools, and heterogeneity between those trials must be reported alongside the
pooled figure.
