# ASD in Cardiology

In cardiology, ASD stands for Atrial Septal Defect: a congenital opening in the
wall between the two upper chambers of the heart. It has nothing to do with the
developmental-psychiatry meaning of the same three letters.

Blood shunts from the left atrium to the right atrium through the defect, which
over years increases the volume load on the right heart and on the pulmonary
circulation. Small defects are frequently asymptomatic and many close on their
own during infancy.

Detection is by echocardiography. A murmur and fixed splitting of the second
heart sound are the classic examination findings, but they are unreliable on
their own, so imaging is what confirms the diagnosis.

Closure is indicated when the shunt is large enough to enlarge the right heart.
Percutaneous device closure has largely replaced open surgical repair for
suitable secundum defects; observational follow-up series report low
complication rates and good long-term outcomes.
